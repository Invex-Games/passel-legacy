Privacy policy

Last updated: 26 November 2026

Passel sends links, notes and files between your own devices. It is run by Declan Smith, in Australia ("we"). Questions about this policy go to contact@invexgames.com.

In short

  • We keep what you send (links, notes and files) so your other devices can show it, until you delete it.
  • From your Google account we keep only its unique id. We don't store your email address or your name.
  • We keep a short record of each device you use Passel on, so we can send it notifications.
  • To show a preview of a link, our server visits the linked page, and any page it redirects to.
  • Notifications go through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging. They say what kind of item arrived and from which of your devices, and for a link its website's name, but never the text of your notes or the names of your files.
  • What you send is not end-to-end encrypted: our server can read it, as it must to deliver it.
  • Passel runs on a server we operate ourselves, not with a cloud hosting company.
  • We don't sell your data, show ads, or put third-party analytics or trackers in our apps or on this site.

What we collect and why

Your account

You sign in with Google. Each request your apps make carries a Google ID token, which our server checks against Google's published keys. From the token we keep one thing: your Google account's unique id, stored with everything you send so that only you can see it. The token also holds your email address and name; your apps show the email address on screen, but our server doesn't store either. We never see your Google password, and signing in gives us no access to anything else in your Google account.

What you send

For a link, we store its address and the name it was sent with, if any; for a note, its text and title, if any; for a file, the file itself, its name, its size and its type, which our server works out from the file's contents. For an image (JPEG, PNG, GIF or WebP) we also store its width and height and make a small preview of it, at most 512 pixels across, with all of the image's metadata, such as location and camera details, left out. The original file is kept exactly as you sent it, metadata included. With each item we store when it was sent, an id of our own, and which of your devices sent it, when the app says.

A file you upload but don't send is deleted after an hour. A file may be at most 25 MB, and your files at most 500 MB together.

Your devices

When you use Passel on a device, the app registers that device with our server. For each device we store a random id the app makes once for its installation, a name for it, which kind of app it is (Android, the Chrome extension or the web app), when it was registered and when it was last seen, and, if you allow notifications, the notification address that Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging gives that app. You can rename and remove your devices. The notification address is never shown back to any app, and is not written to our logs.

Notifications

When you send something from one device, our server asks Firebase Cloud Messaging, a Google service, to show a notification on your other Android devices that have notifications turned on. The message Google carries holds:

  • for a link, the name of the device it came from and the website's address (such as github.com), but not the rest of the link;
  • for a note or a file, only that one arrived and from which device: never the note's text or the file's name;
  • our id for the item and its kind, so that tapping the notification opens it.

Google delivers the message over an encrypted connection, but it is not end-to-end encrypted, so Google can read what it carries. If a device can't be reached, Google keeps the message for up to a day. Google's privacy policy covers how Google handles it. You can turn notifications off in the app or in your phone's settings; signing out stops them for that phone. The Android app does not register devices or turn notifications on yet; this section describes the release that will.

Link previews

When you send a link to a web page, our server fetches that page shortly afterwards to read the title, description and image address the page gives for itself. It only fetches public http and https addresses, never addresses on private networks, follows at most three redirects, reads at most 512 KB, and gives up after three attempts. So one link can mean several requests: one for each attempt, and one more for each redirect along the way. A redirect can lead to another site, such as the real address behind a shortened link, and that site sees our server's requests too. Each request identifies our server as Passel/1.0 (+https://passel.link); nothing about you is sent with it. We store the title, description, image address and the time of the fetch with your link, and delete them with it.

Our server never downloads the preview image. When an app shows a preview, your device loads the image straight from the site that hosts it, as with any image on a web page, so that site can see your device's IP address. Passel's web app sends no referrer with it.

Live updates

While one of your apps is open and visible, it keeps a connection to our server open, so that what you send from one device appears on the others straight away. Nothing extra is stored for it.

Limits on use

To keep Passel usable for everyone, our server counts the requests each account makes (or each IP address, for requests that aren't signed in) over the last minute, in memory only, and checks how much an account uses: up to 5,000 items and 50 MB of links and notes, the file limits above, and 20 devices. The counts aren't stored.

Technical logs

Our server records logs to keep the service running and to fix it: for example which part of the service was called, when, how long it took and what it answered, errors with our own ids for the items and devices involved, and the addresses of pages fetched for previews. They do not contain the text of your notes, the contents of your files, sign-in tokens or notification addresses. They may contain technical details such as the IP address a request came from. They stay on our server and are kept for up to 30 days.

What stays on your devices

  • Web app: your Google sign-in token, in the tab's session storage until you close the tab or sign out, and a note in the browser's local storage that you signed in with Google, so that coming back can offer one-tap sign-in; signing out removes it. Your items are kept in the open tab's memory only. The sign-in page loads Google's sign-in script from Google, which Google's privacy policy covers.
  • Chrome extension: your sign-in token, in the browser's session storage, which is cleared when the browser closes; your email address, so it can sign you back in; and your 50 newest items, so they show at once. Signing out removes the address and the items.
  • Android app: the id of the account last signed in, your 50 newest items, your theme choice and, once notifications arrive, the random installation id it registers with, in the app's own storage; Google's sign-in service on the phone holds the token, and Google Play services hold the notification address. Signing out removes the items and the notification address.

Where it is kept

Passel's server, database and files run on a machine we operate ourselves, in Australia. No cloud hosting provider holds your data. The database and files are backed up every night to storage on the same premises; no copy is kept anywhere else.

How long we keep it

Items are kept until you delete them; nothing expires on its own today. Deleting an item erases it from our database at once, together with its preview, and a deleted file is erased from our disk within seconds; your other devices remove the item as soon as they hear about it. A device's record is kept until you remove the device, and its notification address is also removed when you sign out on it or when Google tells us the app is no longer installed. Backups may hold deleted data for up to 30 days.

The apps can't yet delete your whole account in one step. Until they can, delete your items and devices, or write to contact@invexgames.com and we will delete everything stored under your account.

Who else sees it

  • Nobody but you sees your items through Passel: every item is only ever returned to the account that sent it.
  • Google provides sign-in, and carries notifications as described above.
  • Sites you send links to, and any site such a link redirects to, see the requests from our server described above.
  • We will disclose information if Australian law requires it, for example in answer to a valid order from a court or a request from a law enforcement agency that the law obliges us to meet.

Security

All traffic to our server uses HTTPS. Every request for your items must carry a valid sign-in token, and items and devices are only returned to the account they belong to. Files are served with headers that stop a browser running anything inside them. The preview fetcher refuses addresses on private networks. Our server holds the key it sends notifications with in a file only the service can read.

Your rights

You can see and delete everything you've sent from the apps at any time. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to ask for a copy of your data, to correct it, to have it deleted or to object to how it is used; write to contact@invexgames.com. We handle personal information under the Australian Privacy Principles in the Privacy Act 1988, and only to provide Passel: to sign you in, deliver what you send to your own devices, and keep the service working.

Google, which provides sign-in and carries notifications, may handle the information described above outside Australia, including in the United States. If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, write to us first; if we don't resolve it within 30 days, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner at oaic.gov.au.

Children

Passel is not meant for children under 16.

Changes

When this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. For a change that affects what we collect or who sees it, we'll say so on Passel's home page before it takes effect.
